About Me

From Earth’s past to modern engineering — exploring the ground beneath our feet.

Who I Am

I am an Engineering Geologist working across the UK in ground investigation, earth materials analysis and subsurface characterisation. My work focuses on understanding geological conditions, assessing geohazards, and helping to deliver safe and sustainable engineering solutions.

Academic Background

Before entering industry, I completed an MSc in Past Climate and Environmental Change at Royal Holloway, University of London. I conducted my research at the Centre for Quaternary Research (CQR), one of Europe’s leading hubs for palaeoenvironmental and Quaternary science.

I graduated with a Distinction, specialising in volcanology, geochemical analysis and Quaternary climate reconstruction.

My Research

During my MSc dissertation, I developed a novel machine-learning pipeline for tephra classification. This integrated Random Forests, XGBoost, Flexible Discriminant Analysis and Bayesian age-modelling into a unified, high-accuracy framework for tephra correlation.

The pipeline incorporated:

  • major-element geochemistry
  • compositional data transformations (CLR)
  • ensemble soft-voting models
  • stratigraphic age constraints
  • automated class probability diagnostics

The result was a reproducible classification workflow designed to strengthen analytical tephrochronology and improve correlation reliability in volcanic ash studies.

Read my dissertation or explore the tool here: TephraLink.

Where I Am Now

Today, I bring together both sides of my background — geological research and applied engineering. Whether I’m logging cores onsite, analysing materials, developing classification tools or exploring landscapes shaped by past climates, I’m motivated by the same question:

“How can understanding Earth’s history help us build safely, sustainably and intelligently for the future?”

Beyond Work

Outside of geology, I’m passionate about palaeontology, volcanology, fossil collecting, coding in R and Python, and exploring the landscapes of Scotland. My interests in Earth science — past and present — continue to shape both my career and my curiosity.
