Introduction to Climate Change

Climate change refers to long-term variations in temperature, precipitation, and atmospheric conditions. The Quaternary period offers exceptional insight into natural climate variability and provides context for today’s rapid warming.

The Quaternary Period

Spanning the last 2.6 million years, the Quaternary is marked by repeated glacial and interglacial cycles, major shifts in ecosystems, and the rise of modern humans.

Glacial–Interglacial Cycles

Driven by orbital variations and amplified by climate feedbacks, these cycles define much of Earth’s recent climate behaviour.

How We Know: Climate Proxies

  • Ice cores
  • Marine sediments
  • Speleothems
  • Pollen records
  • Ancient DNA

Major Climate Events of the Quaternary

Key events include the Mid-Pleistocene Transition, the Last Glacial Maximum, deglaciation phases, Holocene climate variability, and the onset of the Anthropocene.

Today's Climate in Quaternary Context

Comparing modern changes to past climates helps us understand the scale and speed of current warming relative to natural variability.
