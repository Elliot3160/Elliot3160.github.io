How to Get Into Engineering Geology

A UK-focused guide for students, graduates and career switchers who want to work with real ground and real projects.

Why Engineering Geology Is a Great Career

Engineering geology is one of the most rewarding and future-proof pathways in the geosciences. It blends geology, engineering, problem-solving and hands-on fieldwork, while supporting major infrastructure, environmental assessment and ground investigation projects across the UK and beyond.

Whether you are a geology student, a recent graduate or someone considering a career change, this guide explains exactly how to get into engineering geology, what skills employers look for, and how to build a CV that stands out in a competitive market.

What Does an Engineering Geologist Actually Do?

Engineering geologists apply geological knowledge to engineering problems. They help designers and project managers understand the ground conditions that structures will be built on, in or through. Typical responsibilities include:

  • Logging soil and rock cores from ground investigation (GI) sites
  • Supervising drilling rigs and ensuring site work is carried out safely
  • Describing ground conditions for foundations, earthworks and slopes
  • Assessing geohazards such as landslides, sinkholes and compressible ground
  • Monitoring groundwater levels and ground gas
  • Producing engineering logs, drawings and technical reports
  • Working closely with civil and geotechnical engineers

In the UK, engineering geologists are essential for projects such as major road and rail upgrades, substations and renewables, land remediation, flood defence schemes and housing developments. If you enjoy fieldwork, interpreting complex ground conditions and solving practical problems, this career is a very good match.

What Qualifications Do You Need?

The good news is that getting into engineering geology is very achievable, especially if you already have a geoscience background.

Undergraduate Degree (Essential)

Most UK employers look for a relevant BSc degree, such as:

  • BSc Geology or Earth Science
  • BSc Engineering Geology or Geotechnical Engineering
  • BSc Environmental Geoscience or Applied Geology

A 2:1 is ideal, but many companies will consider strong 2:2 candidates who can demonstrate field competence and enthusiasm for the sector.

Master’s Degree (Advantageous but Not Always Required)

An MSc is not always essential for entry-level roles, but it can give you a major advantage when competing for graduate positions or fast-track progression. Relevant MSc options include:

  • Engineering Geology or Geotechnical Engineering
  • Applied Environmental Geology or Environmental Management
  • Quaternary Science, Palaeoclimate or Geophysics

If you have a specialist MSc, such as in Past Climate and Environmental Change, that can be a unique selling point – particularly if you can link your research to slope stability, ground conditions, risk assessment or data science.

Key Skills Employers Look For

Beyond your degree, engineering geology employers focus strongly on skills and attitude. Some of the most sought-after attributes include:

  • Fieldwork confidence – comfortable working outdoors, logging cores, making observations and collecting samples.
  • Ground investigation awareness – understanding drilling methods, sampling types and standard tests such as SPTs.
  • Clear written communication – accurate, concise descriptions of strata and conditions in logs and reports.
  • Basic GIS and mapping skills – familiarity with QGIS, ArcGIS or similar tools.
  • Numeracy and data handling – working with lab results, monitoring data and simple calculations.
  • Full UK driving licence – often essential for travelling between sites.

Experience with data analysis, coding (for example in R or Python), or software such as gINT / HoleBASE can set you apart from other applicants and open up more technical career paths.

How to Get Your First Job in Engineering Geology

Breaking into the industry can feel daunting, but most companies are actively looking for motivated graduates. Here are practical steps to improve your chances.

1. Tailor Your CV for Engineering Geology

Make sure your CV highlights relevant field and technical experience. Include:

  • Geological mapping and fieldclass work
  • Any experience logging cores or exposures
  • Ground investigation placements or lab testing roles
  • Software skills: GIS, spreadsheet analysis, coding, gINT/HoleBASE if applicable
  • A short personal statement explaining why you want to work in engineering geology

2. Target the Right Employers

Typical employers in the UK include:

  • Ground investigation contractors
  • Multidisciplinary consultancies and engineering firms
  • Geo-environmental and remediation specialists
  • Local authorities and environmental regulators

Many advertise graduate schemes, but don’t overlook smaller regional firms – they often provide excellent hands-on experience and early responsibility.

3. Expect a Field-Heavy First Year

Most new engineering geologists spend their first 12–24 months gaining core experience in:

  • Core and trial pit logging
  • Site supervision and health & safety
  • Water and gas monitoring
  • Sampling for lab testing

This period is intense but invaluable. It builds the practical understanding of ground conditions that underpins all later design and reporting work.

Engineering Geology Salaries in the UK

Salaries vary by region and employer, but typical ranges in 2025 are:

  • Graduate / entry level: £25,000 – £32,000
  • 2–4 years’ experience: £32,000 – £40,000
  • Senior engineering geologist: £40,000 – £50,000
  • Principal / associate level: £50,000 – £70,000+
  • Technical director: £75,000 – £100,000+ (in larger firms)

Progression is often faster than in purely academic or lab-based roles, especially if you take on responsibility for project management, reporting and client interaction.

How to Stand Out From Other Applicants

Many graduates now apply for engineering geology roles, but relatively few have clear evidence of commitment to the field. You can differentiate yourself by:

  • Showcasing field competence – include photos, mapping projects or short case studies of fieldwork.
  • Building software skills – basic GIS, coding in R/Python, or familiarity with logging software.
  • Writing about geology – starting a blog or portfolio (like this site) demonstrates communication skills and enthusiasm.
  • Highlighting unique strengths – such as machine learning, Quaternary reconstruction, or volcanic hazard work.

If you have experience in palaeoclimate or tephra research, you can position yourself as someone who understands both subsurface processes and data-driven methods – very attractive to forward-looking consultancies.

Career Progression in Engineering Geology

A typical progression pathway in the UK might look like:

Graduate Engineer → Engineering Geologist → Senior → Principal → Technical Lead / Associate → Director

After your first few years of core logging and site work, you can start to specialise in areas such as:

  • Slope stability and landslide risk
  • Geo-environmental assessment and remediation
  • Geohazards and resilience (e.g. earthquakes, subsidence)
  • Geotechnical design support
  • Engineering geomorphology and terrain evaluation
  • Data science and modelling for geotechnics

Many engineering geologists also work towards chartership (CGeol, CEng or similar), which can significantly increase responsibility and earning potential.

Is Engineering Geology a Good Career?

In short: yes. Engineering geology is stable, well paid and intellectually satisfying. It combines time outdoors with technical analysis, and it contributes directly to infrastructure, safety and environmental protection.

You will:

  • Work on real projects that shape landscapes and communities
  • Develop a rare and valuable combination of geological and engineering skills
  • Have clear routes into senior and specialist roles
  • Be part of a profession that is in steady demand

If you enjoy Earth science and want a career that is both practical and impactful, engineering geology is a very strong choice.
